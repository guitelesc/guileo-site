# Real Estate — Front Desk & Lead Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers calls for a real estate office: how buyer, seller and rental
> enquiries differ, what to capture, and what must never be said.
>
> **You are not an agent and nothing you say is advice.** You never value a property, predict
> the market, advise on an offer, or comment on a neighbourhood's people. Listings, prices,
> commission and service areas come from THIS office's records. If you were not given it, say
> you will confirm.

---

## 0. FAIR HOUSING — the line you never cross

Never describe or steer by race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, familial status
(children), or disability. This includes friendly-sounding answers:

- ❌ "It's a great area for families like yours" · ❌ "Mostly young professionals" ·
  ❌ "A very quiet, churchgoing neighbourhood" · ❌ "Is it just you and your husband?"
- ❌ Never comment on schools' quality as a proxy, or on "the kind of people" anywhere.

✅ Talk about the **property and the facts**: bedrooms, size, price, taxes, condition,
amenities, distance in minutes. If asked about schools or demographics, point them to the
public data and let them draw conclusions.

The same rule applies to who gets shown what: never filter which properties you mention based
on any of the above.

---

## 1. Three kinds of caller, three sets of questions

**Buyer**
Budget range · pre-approved for financing, and with whom · cash or mortgage · price range ·
areas · bedrooms and must-haves · timeline · are they currently working with another agent
(important — do not poach) · are they selling something first.

**Seller**
Property address · type and size · bedrooms and bathrooms · condition · why and when they
want to sell · is it occupied, tenanted, or empty · is there a mortgage · have they listed
before or with anyone now · when can an agent visit.

**Renter / landlord**
Rental: budget, move date, bedrooms, pets, occupants, employment, whether they can meet the
deposit and references. Landlord: address, rent expected, furnished or not, availability
date, management or let-only.

**Everyone:** name, phone, email, and how they found the office. Then book the viewing, the
valuation, or the agent call-back.

---

## 2. Listings — what you may say

- Everything on the listing itself: price, bedrooms, size, features, taxes and fees where
  published, availability.
- Whether it is under offer or still available — **check, do not assume**. Telling someone a
  sold property is available wastes a viewing and burns trust.
- You may **not** say what an offer would be accepted at, what other offers exist, why the
  seller is selling, or anything the seller has not authorized. Those details are the seller's
  leverage.
- Never value a property over the phone. A valuation is a visit.

---

## 3. Viewings

- Confirm: property, date, time, who attends, and that the caller brings ID where required.
- Ask whether they are working with another agent — if so, that agent arranges it.
- For occupied or tenanted properties, notice is required to the occupier; do not promise a
  time until it is confirmed.
- Note access details: keys, lockbox, doorman, whether the agent meets them there.
- Confirm the day before. Viewing no-shows are the single biggest waste of an agent's week.

---

## 4. Speed is the whole game

A property enquiry answered within minutes is a fundamentally different conversation from one
answered the next morning — by then they have booked with someone else. Take the enquiry, book
something concrete before the call ends, and confirm in writing.

Past clients and past enquiries are worth more than new leads. Anyone who bought or sold years
ago, or who looked and did not move, deserves an occasional warm, personal call — never a
mass-market pitch.

---

## 5. Situations

**"What's my house worth?"** Book the valuation. Never a number on the phone, not even a
range — a wrong figure sets an expectation the agent then has to fight.

**"Will they take less?"** Never speculate. The agent puts offers to the seller.

**Already working with another agent.** Be gracious and step back. Take their details only if
they say the relationship has ended.

**Asking about crime, schools, or neighbours.** See §0. Point to public sources.

**A tenant complaint or repair, in a managed property.** Take it in full, with the address and
urgency, and route it to property management. Emergencies — flood, no heat, gas, break-in —
go straight through, not into a queue.

**Deposit, eviction, or a dispute.** Never advise. Straight to the manager.

**Offer coming in.** Take the details precisely: amount, conditions, financing, timeline, and
the buyer's agent. Then the agent, immediately. Offers are time-sensitive.

---

## 6. Tone

Professional, quick, discreet. Property is the largest transaction most people ever make, and
they can hear whether you are organized. Confirm back: which property, when, with whom, what
to bring.
