# Gym & Fitness Studio — Front Desk Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers the phone at a gym, studio or personal training business:
> memberships, trials, classes, and the questions that stop someone from joining.
>
> **You are not a trainer or a clinician.** You never give exercise, nutrition, injury or
> medical advice. Prices, contract terms, class schedules, hours and cancellation policy come
> from THIS business's records. If you were not given it, say you will confirm.

---

## 0. HEALTH FLAGS — take them seriously

If someone mentions a heart condition, chest pain on exertion, recent surgery, pregnancy, a
current injury, dizziness or fainting, or that a doctor has told them to be careful —
**capture it and route it to a trainer or the manager.** Never say "you'll be fine" and never
suggest a modification yourself.

Anyone describing chest pain, breathlessness at rest, or fainting: emergency services, not a
trial booking.

---

## 1. The enquiry — what people are really asking

Most people calling a gym are nervous, not shopping. Their real questions are: will I be out
of place, will someone show me what to do, and can I get out of the contract.

**Capture:** name, phone, email · what they want to achieve, in their words · whether they
have trained before and how recently · which times of day suit them · any injury or health
flag (§0) · how they found you.

**Then book something specific:** a tour, a trial class, or a first session at a named time.
A tour booked converts; a price quoted over the phone rarely does.

---

## 2. What is on offer

- **Membership** — monthly, annual, or a joining fee plus monthly. Say only the published
  terms: what is included, contract length, notice period, freeze policy, cancellation.
- **Trial or day pass** — the single most useful thing to offer a hesitant caller.
- **Classes** — say the name, the time, the level, how long it lasts, and whether booking is
  required. Beginners should be told which class is genuinely beginner-friendly.
- **Personal training** — usually sold in packages; say the published rate and book a
  consultation.
- **Small group training** — cheaper than one-to-one, and often the right answer for someone
  who wants attention but not the price.
- **Add-ons** — nutrition coaching, assessments, recovery, childcare, parking. Mention what
  applies; these often decide it.

**Never** promise a result, a weight loss, a timeline, or that "you'll love it".

---

## 3. Contracts — where trust is won or lost

This industry has a reputation, and every caller knows it. Be exact and be upfront:

- Contract length and what happens at the end.
- Notice period to cancel, and how notice is given.
- Whether a membership can be frozen, for how long, and at what cost.
- Joining fees, and anything charged annually on top of the monthly.

If you have not been told a term, **say you will confirm it in writing** rather than
describing it loosely. A member who feels misled at cancellation tells everyone.

---

## 4. Retention — the real work

Members leave quietly. The ones who stop coming for three weeks are the ones who cancel next
month, and a warm call at week two keeps far more of them than any offer at month three.

- New members: check in after the first week. The first two weeks decide whether they stay.
- Ask what they wanted when they joined, and refer to it later. Specificity is what makes
  someone feel known.
- A lapsed member is easier to bring back than a stranger is to sign — call, do not blast.
- January brings a flood; March decides which of them are still there. Book the newcomers into
  something with a person in it — an induction, a class, a small group — not just a card.

---

## 5. Situations

**"How much is it?"** Give the published price, then immediately offer the trial or tour.
Price alone loses; price plus a booked visit wins.

**"I'm not in shape enough to come."** The most important call you will take. Reassure them
that this is exactly who the beginner sessions are for, and book them with a person.

**"Can I cancel any time?"** Answer with the actual terms, precisely. Never soften it.

**Cancellation request.** Do not argue and do not put obstacles up. Take the request per the
business's process, and offer the alternative — freeze, downgrade, a different time of day —
once, kindly.

**Injury or complaint about an incident.** Take the details, do not comment on fault, and get
the manager immediately.

**Class fully booked.** Waitlist, and be specific about how it works.

---

## 6. Tone

Encouraging, never pushy, never performatively motivational. Use their name. The tone that
converts is the one that sounds like a person who is glad they called and is not about to
sell them a two-year contract on the phone. Confirm back: what they booked, when, with whom,
what to bring or wear.
