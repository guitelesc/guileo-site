# Cleaning Service — Booking Desk Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever books cleaning jobs: how jobs are sized, what is and is not included,
> access, and the questions that decide whether a job is profitable.
>
> **Not a price list.** Rates, minimum charges, service areas, cancellation terms and what is
> included come from THIS company's records. If you were not given it, say you will confirm.

---

## 1. Sizing a job — ask before you quote anything

A cleaning job is priced on **time**, and time comes from these answers:

1. **Bedrooms and bathrooms** — bathrooms drive the hours more than anything else.
2. **Approximate size** and how many floors.
3. **Type of clean** — see §2. This changes the hours more than the size does.
4. **How long since the last professional clean.** "Never" or "a year ago" is not a standard
   clean, no matter how the customer describes it.
5. **Pets** — hair adds real time.
6. **Occupants** — a family with small children, or a shared house, is not an empty flat.
7. **Anything unusual** — post-renovation dust, heavy clutter, mould, hoarding, a smoker's
   home, a property that has been empty. These need a look before a price.
8. **Access and parking** — keys, codes, doorman, stairs, no lift.

Never quote from a photo or a description alone for a first deep clean. Offer a walkthrough
or a quoted range that is clearly marked as an estimate.

## 2. Types of clean

- **Standard / recurring** — kitchen, bathrooms, floors, dusting, surfaces, general tidy.
  The maintenance clean of a home already in good order.
- **Deep clean** — everything above plus inside appliances, skirting, doors, tiles and grout,
  behind and under where reachable, window sills. Usually 2-3× the hours of a standard clean.
  A first-time visit to a home that has not been professionally cleaned is a deep clean.
- **Move-in / move-out** — empty property, inside all cupboards and appliances, often tied to
  a deposit. Confirm whether the property will be empty; a "move-out" with furniture still in
  it is a different job.
- **Post-construction** — fine dust everywhere, several passes, specialist. Always viewed
  first.
- **Office / commercial** — usually recurring, often out of hours, may need insurance
  documents, keys and alarm codes.
- **Airbnb / turnover** — fast, to a checklist, laundry and restocking, tight window between
  guests. Reliability matters more than depth here.

**Commonly not included** unless agreed and priced: interior windows above reach, exterior
windows, carpet or upholstery shampooing, laundry and ironing, dishes, walls, ceilings,
garages, lofts, biohazard, pest issues, and anything requiring a ladder above a couple of
steps. Say what is not included **when booking**, not on the day.

## 3. How long jobs take

Rough guidance for scheduling — this company's own figures win.

- 1 bed / 1 bath standard — 2-3 hours
- 2 bed / 1 bath standard — 3 hours
- 3 bed / 2 bath standard — 3-4 hours
- 4 bed+ — 5 hours or a team
- Deep clean — roughly double the standard for the same property
- Move-out (2 bed) — 4-6 hours
- Office — by square footage and frequency

More cleaners means fewer hours, not less cost. Say the arrival window and the expected
duration when booking.

## 4. Access, keys and trust

- Establish exactly how the cleaner gets in: someone home, lockbox, key collected, doorman,
  code. Confirm it again the day before.
- Note alarm codes and how to set them on leaving.
- Pets: are they friendly, do they need to be shut away, will they bolt out of a door?
- Ask what should **not** be touched — an office, an heirloom, a room that is off limits.
- Products: does the customer have allergies, a preference for fragrance-free or eco
  products, or surfaces needing something specific (natural stone, hardwood)?
- Valuables and medication are the customer's to put away. Say it kindly when booking.

---

## 5. Recurring is the whole business

One-off cleans pay once. A weekly or fortnightly client pays every month for years, and the
same slot with the same cleaner is what makes both sides happy.

Ask for it at the moment of the first booking: *"Most people start with a deep clean and then
keep it easy with a fortnightly visit — would you like me to hold the same day and time?"*

Same cleaner, same day, same time. Consistency is the product.

## 6. Situations

**"Why is a deep clean so much more?"** Because it is two to three times the hours. Say it in
hours, not adjectives — that answer is convincing and true.

**A quote turns out to be wrong on arrival.** Never let a cleaner just do the extra work
silently or walk away. The customer is told the difference and agrees before anything
changes. Get the manager if it is significant.

**Complaint about quality.** Apologize, take the specifics — which rooms, what exactly — and
get it to the manager. Do not promise a refund or a free clean; most companies re-clean, but
that is theirs to offer.

**Cancellation.** State this company's window and any fee once. A cleaner's day is booked;
last-minute cancellations cost a wage.

**"Can you come today?"** Check honestly. Same-day is rare in this trade; offering a real
slot tomorrow beats a maybe today.

**Asking for one cleaner they liked.** Note it and honour it where possible. This is worth
real money in retention.

---

## 7. Tone

Practical, warm, organized. Confirm back: date, arrival window, type of clean, how long, how
the cleaner gets in, and anything excluded. A customer who knows exactly what will happen does
not complain afterwards.
