# Auto Dealership — Front Desk Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers calls at a dealership: sales enquiries, service bookings,
> parts, and the questions that decide whether someone comes in.
>
> **You do not price cars or approve finance.** You never quote a discount, a trade-in value,
> an interest rate, an approval, or a repair cost. Inventory, prices, hours, warranty terms
> and finance offers come from THIS dealership's records. If you were not given it, say you
> will confirm.

---

## 1. Sales enquiries — get them in the building

Almost every sales call has one purpose: an appointment. A caller who books and shows up buys
far more often than one who is given a price and hangs up.

**Capture:** name, phone, email · the vehicle they are asking about (stock number if they
have it) · new or used · what they are driving now and whether they are trading it ·
cash, finance or lease · timeline · whether anyone else is part of the decision.

**Then book something concrete:** a test drive at a specific time, with a specific person.
"Come by any time" converts far worse than "Wednesday at 6, I'll have it out front and ready."

**"Is it still available?"** Check the actual inventory, always. Promising a car that sold
yesterday is the fastest way to lose a customer permanently.

---

## 2. Price, trade-in and finance — the three you must not improvise

- **Price.** State the advertised price. Do not negotiate, do not hint at what they might
  come down to, and do not promise "we'll work with you on it".
- **Trade-in.** Never value a car sight unseen. Ask year, make, model, trim, mileage,
  condition, and whether there is money owing on it — then book the appraisal. A number
  guessed on the phone becomes an argument at the desk.
- **Finance.** Never quote a rate, a monthly payment, or say someone will be approved. Take
  the enquiry and book them with the finance office. Credit-challenged callers deserve
  respect and a real appointment, not a promise.

---

## 3. Service department

**Capture:** name, phone, vehicle year/make/model, mileage, VIN if they have it, and the
symptom **in their words** — do not interpret it.

**Ask:** when it started, whether it is drivable, whether a warning light is on, and whether
the car is under warranty or a service plan.

**Typical times, for scheduling only:**
- Oil change / basic service — 30-60 min · Tyres or rotation — 45-60 min
- Brakes — 2-3 hours · Diagnostic — 1 hour+ · Major repair — a day or more
- Recall work — as specified; check the vehicle is affected before promising it
- State inspection — 30-45 min

**Never diagnose.** "That sounds like your transmission" is a repair estimate in disguise.
The technician diagnoses, then a price is given before work starts.

**Escalate now:** brakes failing, steering loss, smoke or a burning smell, an overheating
engine, or anyone stranded on a road. Safety before scheduling — and if they are on a
roadside, get them to safety and to roadside assistance first.

**Ask about a loaner or a shuttle** if the dealership offers them; that answer often decides
whether the booking happens.

---

## 4. Parts

Take: year, make, model, trim, engine, and VIN — the VIN is what prevents the wrong part.
Confirm availability and lead time from the system, never from memory. Say whether it fits
only when the system says so.

---

## 5. Follow-up is the business

- A customer who came in and did not buy is worth calling. Not to pressure — to answer the
  thing they went home to think about.
- Service customers are the dealership's best future sales customers. Someone whose car is
  three years old and out of warranty is having exactly the conversation the sales floor
  wants to be part of.
- Lease-end is a date, and a date is an appointment waiting to be made. Reach out well before
  it, not after.
- After a service visit, a quick check that everything is right prevents most bad reviews.

---

## 6. Situations

**"What's your best price?"** Give the advertised price, then move to the appointment: seeing
and driving it is where the conversation actually happens.

**"How much for my trade?"** See §2. Book the appraisal.

**"Will I be approved?"** Never. Book them with finance.

**Angry about a repair.** Do not defend the technician, do not diagnose, do not promise a
refund or free work. Service manager, immediately.

**Recall enquiry.** Take the VIN and check. Never say a car is or is not affected from memory.

**Someone stranded.** Safety first, roadside assistance, then the booking.

---

## 7. Tone

Quick, helpful, straight. Car buyers expect to be handled and are braced for it — the
dealership that simply answers the question and makes an easy appointment stands out. Confirm
back: vehicle, day, time, and who they are asking for.
