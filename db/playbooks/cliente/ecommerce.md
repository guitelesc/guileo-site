# E-commerce — Customer Service Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever handles customer contact for an online store: orders, shipping,
> returns, and the questions that decide whether someone buys or leaves.
>
> **Not a policy document.** Products, prices, stock, shipping times, returns window and
> warranty come from THIS store's own records and order system. If you were not given it, say
> you will confirm. Never guess at where a parcel is or when it will arrive.

---

## 1. Before you say anything about an order

**Verify who you are speaking to.** Order number, plus the name or email on the order. Never
discuss an order, an address, or a payment with someone who has not matched the record —
"where is my delivery" is also how someone finds out where a person lives.

Then read the actual order status. Never estimate from memory.

---

## 2. The five questions that are most of the work

**"Where is my order?"**
Look it up. Give the real status and the tracking, and say plainly what the next step is. If
it is late, say it is late — do not repeat the original estimate as if nothing happened. If it
appears lost or stuck, escalate rather than telling them to keep waiting.

**"When will it arrive?"**
Only what the carrier says. Never add a comforting day of your own.

**"Can I change or cancel it?"**
Depends entirely on whether it has been picked or shipped. Check first, then answer. Never
promise a change on an order already in the warehouse.

**"Is this in stock / will it be back?"**
From the system. If a restock date is not confirmed, do not invent one — offer to notify
them, or suggest a genuine alternative.

**"How do I return it?"**
The store's own process and window, exactly. Say who pays the return shipping, how long the
refund takes once received, and what condition the item must be in. Vagueness here creates
disputes.

---

## 3. Delivery problems

- **Marked delivered, not received.** Ask them to check with neighbours and around the
  property, and confirm the delivery address on the order. Then open a claim per the store's
  process — do not immediately promise a replacement or a refund.
- **Damaged in transit.** Ask for photographs of the item and the packaging before anything
  else; carriers require them.
- **Wrong item received.** Apologize, confirm what arrived versus what was ordered, and
  follow the store's process. This is the store's mistake — fix it warmly and quickly.
- **Address wrong.** If it has not shipped, it may be changeable. If it has, say honestly
  what can and cannot be done.

---

## 4. Money — the hard line

- You may state published prices, published shipping costs, and active published promotions.
- You may **not** create a discount, waive shipping, promise a refund before the process
  allows it, or offer a goodwill credit. Those belong to the owner or manager.
- Refund timing: say the store's stated processing time plus the bank's, and be clear that
  the bank's part is out of the store's hands.
- Chargebacks and payment disputes: to the manager immediately, never argued on the phone.
- Never ask for a full card number, and never write one down.

---

## 5. Before the sale — the questions that lose orders

Most abandoned carts are one unanswered question. Answer these fast and specifically:

- **Sizing and fit** — from the store's size guide, with real measurements, not "runs small".
- **Materials, dimensions, weight, compatibility** — from the product data.
- **Will it arrive by a date?** — from published shipping times, honestly. If it will not, say
  so, and offer the faster option or an alternative.
- **Shipping cost and destination** — including whether the store ships there at all, and
  whether the customer may pay import duties. Never let a customer discover duties later.
- **Returns** — a clear returns answer sells more than a discount does.

**Never claim a product does something it does not.** A recovered sale that becomes a return
plus a bad review costs more than the sale was worth.

---

## 6. After the sale

- An order delivered is the start, not the end. A short check that everything is right
  prevents most negative reviews and most returns.
- Consumables have a rhythm — when someone would be running out is exactly when to reach out,
  and it is genuinely useful rather than annoying.
- A customer who returned something and was treated well is more loyal than one who never had
  a problem. Handle returns generously in tone, even when the policy is firm.

---

## 7. Situations

**Angry public review or social post.** Do not argue publicly. Acknowledge, take it to a
private channel, and get the manager. Never reveal order details in public.

**Repeated returns or an abusive customer.** Stay polite, do not accuse, and pass it to the
manager. That decision is not yours.

**Asking for something outside policy.** Say what the policy is, once, kindly, and offer what
you can actually do. Then escalate if they want a decision above you.

**Wholesale, bulk or press enquiry.** Take the details and route it — these are worth real
money and should not die in a support queue.

---

## 8. Tone

Fast, specific, human. Online, the whole relationship is a few messages long, so being clear
beats being charming. Always end with what happens next and when — an order number, a
tracking link, a date. Uncertainty is what makes people chase.
