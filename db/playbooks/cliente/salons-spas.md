# Salon & Spa — Front Desk Knowledge

> This is craft knowledge for whoever answers the phone at a salon or spa: what the services
> are, how long they take, when a client is due back, what to warn them about, and how to
> handle the situations that come up every day.
>
> **It is NOT a policy document.** Prices, hours, cancellation windows, deposit amounts and
> staff names come from THIS business's own records. When something here says "the usual"
> or "commonly", treat it as background, never as this salon's rule. If you were not given
> the number, say you'll confirm it — never guess a price, a policy or a stylist's schedule.

---

## 1. Services — what they are and how long they take

Typical chair time. Always book the duration this business has on file for the service; the
ranges below are only to help you recognize when something sounds wrong.

**Hair**
- Women's cut & style — 45-60 min
- Men's cut — 30 min
- Blow-dry / style only — 30-45 min
- Root touch-up (single process) — 1h30-2h
- All-over colour — 2h
- Partial highlights — 2h-2h30
- Full highlights — 3h
- Balayage / ombré — 3h-4h
- Colour correction — 4h+ **and a consultation first, always**
- Keratin / smoothing — 2h30-3h30
- Perm / relaxer — 2h-3h
- Deep-conditioning or gloss add-on — +20-30 min

**Nails**
- Manicure — 30-45 min · Gel manicure — 45-60 min
- Pedicure — 45-60 min · Gel/spa pedicure — 60-75 min
- Full set (acrylic/gel-X) — 90 min · Fill — 60 min
- Removal — +20-30 min, and it is *not* free time — book it

**Lashes & brows**
- Classic lash full set — 2h · Volume full set — 2h30
- Lash fill — 60-75 min (only if enough extensions remain — see §4)
- Lash lift — 45-60 min · Brow lamination — 45 min
- Brow wax/shape — 15-20 min · Tint — +15 min

**Skin & body**
- Express facial — 30 min · Classic facial — 60 min · Advanced/peel — 75-90 min
- Swedish or deep-tissue massage — 60 or 90 min
- Hot stone — 90 min · Prenatal massage — 60 min (see contraindications)
- Body wrap / scrub — 60 min
- Waxing: lip/brow 15 min · underarm 15 min · half leg 30 min · full leg 60 min ·
  bikini 30 min · Brazilian 45 min

**Booking rules that prevent a ruined day**
- Long chemical services need a start time that leaves the stylist a full block — never
  squeeze a 4h balayage into a 3h gap.
- Add cleanup/turnover time after facials, massages and pedicures; it is part of the booking,
  not a gap.
- A first-time colour client is a consultation plus service, not a service alone.

## 2. When a client is due back

Offering the next appointment at the right moment is the single most useful thing a front
desk does. Standard intervals:

| Service | Comes back in |
|---|---|
| Root touch-up / grey coverage | 4-6 weeks |
| Highlights / balayage | 8-12 weeks |
| Gloss refresh | 4-6 weeks |
| Haircut (short/tapered) | 4-6 weeks |
| Haircut (long) | 8-12 weeks |
| Lash fill | 2-3 weeks |
| Brow wax / tint | 3-4 weeks |
| Gel manicure | 2-3 weeks |
| Acrylic fill | 2-3 weeks |
| Pedicure | 4-6 weeks |
| Facial (maintenance) | 4 weeks |
| Massage (maintenance) | 4 weeks |

Say it as care, not as a sale: *"Your colour will start showing at the root around week five —
want me to hold your usual Thursday now so you get the time you like?"*

## 3. What to tell them before they come

Prep matters — the wrong prep costs the salon the appointment.

- **Colour:** come with clean, dry hair, not freshly washed the same morning; no heavy oils.
  Wear a dark top. Allow the full time — colour cannot be rushed.
- **Keratin/smoothing:** do not wash or tie the hair for the number of days the stylist tells
  you afterwards.
- **Lash appointment:** arrive with no eye makeup and no mascara; do not curl lashes that day.
  Caffeine can make eyes flutter — light on coffee.
- **Facial:** no retinol, acids or strong actives for a few days before; no waxing the same
  week; come without makeup if possible.
- **Waxing:** hair needs to be about a quarter-inch; no exfoliating or sun the day before or
  after; a recent retinol course is a reason to reschedule.
- **Massage:** hydrate, avoid a heavy meal right before; mention any injury or surgery.
- **Spray tan / body treatment:** exfoliate the day before, come with clean skin and no
  lotion.
- **Everyone:** arriving late shortens the service — it does not move the next client.

## 4. Questions you must not answer alone

These need the stylist, the therapist or the owner. Take the details, be warm, and hand it
over — a confident wrong answer here is worse than a short wait.

- **Colour correction, box-dye, bleach on virgin or damaged hair, going more than two levels
  lighter.** Always a consultation first. Never quote a flat price for a correction.
- **Patch test.** Many colour and lash services require one a set time before the
  appointment. Ask whether they have had this service here before, and if there is any doubt,
  book the patch test — do not decide it yourself.
- **Lash fill vs full set.** A fill only works if enough extensions remain (commonly around
  half, after roughly three weeks). If they are past that, or unsure, book as a full set or
  flag it for the artist rather than promising a fill price.
- **Skin conditions, active acne, cold sores, sunburn, recent injectables or laser,
  medications like isotretinoin.** These change or postpone facials, peels and waxing.
- **Pregnancy.** Some massages, treatments and products are avoided or need a prenatal
  provider; first trimester is often declined. Never wave it through.
- **Allergies and reactions** — capture them and pass them on, every time.
- **Anything medical.** You are not qualified to advise, and neither is the salon.

## 5. The situations that come up every day

**"Can you fit me in today?"**
Check the real calendar. If there is nothing, offer the waitlist and be specific about how it
works: they get a message the moment something frees up, first to reply gets it. A vague
"we'll try" wastes a client who would have taken a Tuesday.

**Someone cancels and leaves a hole.**
That gap is money. Offer it to the waitlist first, then to clients who are already overdue for
their usual service. Same-day, short notice — keep the message short and easy to say yes to.

**"How much is it?"**
Quote only from this business's price list, and only for the exact service. Levels matter: a
senior stylist and a junior are different prices. If the service is not on the list, or the
price depends on hair length, density or a consultation, say exactly that and offer to
confirm — never invent a figure, never round.

**They want a specific person.**
Honour it. A client who only sees one stylist is a loyal client — offer that stylist's next
opening before offering anyone else, and say when they are next free rather than pushing a
substitute.

**Late cancel or no-show.**
Follow this business's stated window and deposit rule. Be matter-of-fact and kind: state the
policy once, do not argue it, and offer the next available time. If you have not been told
the policy, do not improvise one.

**Running late.**
Ask how late. Past a certain point the service must be shortened or moved — say which,
clearly, before they set off.

**Group or bridal.**
Ask the date, the number of people, and which services each person wants. These need several
providers at the same time and are usually booked with a deposit. Get the details and confirm
availability properly — never promise a large group on the spot.

**Complaint about a result.**
Apologize sincerely, do not diagnose what went wrong, and do not offer a refund, a discount, a
free service or a redo. Get them to the owner or manager quickly. Concessions are the owner's
to give.

**Gift cards.**
Sell them, and for a balance or an expiry, check the record. Never estimate a balance.

## 6. Add-ons worth mentioning (once, never twice)

Suggested in context, these help the client and lift the ticket. Mentioned twice, they are
pressure — say it once and let it go.

- Colour → gloss or toner ("keeps it from going brassy")
- Any hair service → deep-conditioning or bond treatment, especially after lightening
- Blow-dry → scalp massage
- Manicure → paraffin, or gel instead of regular for a two-week hold
- Pedicure → callus treatment or extended massage
- Facial → LED, extractions, or a scalp/neck add-on
- Massage → aromatherapy, hot stones, extend 60 → 90 min
- Brow wax → tint or lamination
- Lash fill → brow shape at the same visit
- Before a holiday or event → book the touch-up now for the week before

Retail follows the service: whatever the stylist used and recommended, and a reminder when a
bottle they bought would be running out.

## 7. Slow times and busy times

- Quiet: January and February, mid-week mornings, the first week after a holiday.
- Busy: prom and graduation season, weddings from late spring, the fortnight before major
  holidays, every Friday and Saturday.
- Fill quiet windows actively; protect prime slots. In peak weeks, prime time books itself —
  that is when to encourage booking ahead, not to discount.

---

## 8. Tone

Warm, quick, unhurried in manner but efficient with time. Use the client's name. If they have
been here before, act like it — their usual service, their usual person, their usual day.
Confirm the details back to them at the end: service, day, time, who with. Then let them go.
