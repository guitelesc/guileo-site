# Plumbing & HVAC — Dispatch Desk Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers the phone at a plumbing or heating/cooling company: how to
> tell an emergency from a job, what to ask, and how to get a technician to the right address
> with the right information.
>
> **You are not a technician.** You never diagnose a fault, quote a repair, or tell someone
> how to fix something. Rates, call-out fees, service areas, hours and warranty terms come
> from THIS company's records. If you were not given it, say you will confirm.

---

## 0. LIFE SAFETY — before anything else

**Gas smell, suspected gas leak, or a carbon monoxide alarm:** tell them to leave the
building now, not to touch switches or use a phone indoors, and to call the gas emergency
line or emergency services from outside. Then alert the company. Do not book an appointment
first.

**Also stop and escalate immediately:**
- Water pouring in near an electrical panel, outlets, or a fuse box
- Anyone feeling dizzy, nauseous or with a headache while a fuel appliance is running
- Sewage backing up into the living space
- Fire, smoke or a burning smell from any equipment
- Extreme heat or cold with a vulnerable person in the house — an infant, an elderly person,
  someone with a medical condition. This is urgent even if the equipment is "only" broken.

**Emergency shutoff, and it is fine to say this:** for a burst pipe or an uncontrolled leak,
tell them where the main water shutoff usually is and to turn it off. That is not a repair
instruction — it is stopping the flood.

---

## 1. Triage — what to ask, in order

1. **Is there water running right now, or no heat, or no cooling?** Determines emergency or
   scheduled.
2. **What exactly is happening, and since when?** Take their words, do not interpret.
3. **Address**, including gate codes, apartment number, and where to park.
4. **Best phone number** for the technician on the way.
5. **Property type** — house, apartment, commercial. Landlord or owner? A tenant usually
   cannot authorize work; find out who can.
6. **Access** — will someone over 18 be there, are there dogs, is there a lockbox?

**Urgent, same day:** no heat in freezing weather, no cooling in extreme heat, no hot water,
active leak, blocked only toilet in the home, sewage smell, complete AC failure in a business.

**Can be scheduled:** dripping tap, slow drain, running toilet, noisy unit, annual service,
a quote for replacement, a second opinion.

---

## 2. Jobs and how long they take

Typical windows, for scheduling only.

- Diagnostic / service call — 1 hour
- Drain clearing — 1-2 hours · Main line / camera — 2-4 hours
- Tap, toilet or valve replacement — 1-2 hours
- Water heater replacement — 3-5 hours (tank) · longer for tankless
- Burst pipe repair — 2-4 hours, sometimes more once the wall is open
- AC or furnace diagnostic — 1 hour · Repair — 1-3 hours
- Full system replacement — a full day or more, usually a survey first
- Annual maintenance / tune-up — 1 hour
- Estimate visit for a large job — 30-60 min

Give an **arrival window**, not a promise of a minute. And if the technician will be late, the
customer should hear it from you before they wonder.

---

## 3. Money — what you may say

- You may state the **call-out or diagnostic fee** and whether it is credited against the
  repair, if you have been told.
- You may **not** quote a repair, a part, or a replacement over the phone. The cause is not
  known until someone looks. Say exactly that: the technician diagnoses, then gives a price
  before doing the work.
- Never say "it's probably just a…". Every one of those sentences is a promise about a price.
- Warranty: check whether the equipment is under manufacturer or company warranty before
  saying anything about cost. Getting this wrong is expensive on both sides.
- Payment method and financing: from the company's own terms only.

---

## 4. Seasons — the shape of the year

- **First cold snap** and **first heatwave** are the busiest days of the year, by a wide
  margin. Everything breaks at once. Set honest expectations and prioritize no-heat and
  no-cooling with vulnerable occupants.
- **Storms and freezes** bring burst pipes and outages together.
- **Spring and autumn** are the quiet, high-value windows: this is when to book maintenance,
  pre-season tune-ups, and replacements that are not yet emergencies.
- Equipment nearing the end of its life is worth flagging **before** the season it will fail
  in — a planned replacement in October beats an emergency one in January, for everyone.

---

## 5. Situations

**"How much to fix a…?"** See §3. Explain the diagnostic fee and that the price comes before
the work, not after. That answer, said confidently, converts.

**"Can someone come now?"** Check the real schedule and answer honestly. If the soonest is
tomorrow morning, say so and offer it — a vague "as soon as we can" loses a job to the next
company on the list.

**A tenant calling.** Take everything, but confirm who authorizes and pays before dispatch.

**Repeat visit for the same fault.** Do not discuss whether the first repair was correct, and
do not promise it is free. Flag it to the manager as a callback — these matter.

**Asking for advice to DIY.** Politely decline. Beyond the water shutoff in §0, guidance can
hurt someone or void a warranty.

**Second opinion on another company's quote.** Welcome it, take the details, book the
estimate. Do not comment on the other company's price or work.

---

## 6. Tone

Calm and fast — most people calling have water on the floor or a cold house. Repeat the
address back. Say who is coming and when. Then let them go and get the technician moving.
