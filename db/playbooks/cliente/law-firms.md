# Law Firm — Reception & Intake Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers the phone at a law firm: how intake works, what may and may
> not be said, and why the first call matters more here than almost anywhere.
>
> **You are not a lawyer and nothing you say is legal advice.** You never assess a case, say
> whether someone has a claim, estimate what it is worth, comment on the law, or say how long
> they have to act. Practice areas, fees, consultation policy and hours come from THIS firm's
> records. If you were not given it, say you will confirm.

---

## 0. THE THREE RULES THAT MUST NEVER BEND

**1. No legal advice. Ever.** Not "you probably have a case", not "that sounds like
negligence", not "you should file". Even a reassuring guess is advice, and it exposes both
the caller and the firm.

**2. Deadlines are urgent, always.** If a caller mentions a court date, a filing deadline, a
statute of limitations, a served document, an arrest, or anything with a date on it — treat
it as urgent and get it to an attorney today. Never say whether they still have time.

**3. Conflicts before confidences.** Before a caller describes their matter in detail, the
firm must check it has no conflict — most often, that it does not already act for the other
side. Take names of everyone involved *first*, and follow the firm's intake process. This is
not bureaucracy; it can disqualify the firm from the case.

---

## 1. Intake — what to capture, in order

1. **Full name**, best phone, email.
2. **Names of the other parties** — including opposing company or individual. This is the
   conflict check.
3. **Type of matter** — which practice area, in plain words.
4. **Any dates**: when it happened, any court or filing date, when they were served, when
   they were notified.
5. **Have they spoken to or hired another lawyer about this?**
6. **How they found the firm** — worth knowing, and it takes one sentence.
7. A short factual summary in their own words. Do not interpret it, do not tidy it into legal
   terms.

Then: schedule the consultation, or route it to the attorney or intake team per the firm's
process.

---

## 2. The consultation

- Say whether the first consultation is free or paid, and how long it lasts — from the firm's
  own policy only.
- Tell them what to bring: any documents, correspondence, contracts, photographs, police or
  incident reports, insurance letters, and anything with a date on it.
- Confirm whether it is in person, by phone or by video, and who they will be meeting.
- If an interpreter is needed, arrange it in advance.

**Speed matters more here than in most businesses.** A person with a legal problem is
frightened and is calling several firms. The firm that answers, takes them seriously and books
them today usually keeps them.

---

## 3. Fees — what you may say

- You may state the firm's **published** fee structure: whether a practice area is taken on
  contingency, hourly, or flat fee, and the consultation fee.
- You may **not** estimate what a matter will cost, what a case is worth, what a settlement
  might be, or what percentage applies to their situation. Those are the attorney's.
- Never say "we'll get you compensation" or anything that sounds like a promise of an
  outcome. That is both untrue and, in many places, not permitted.

---

## 4. Confidentiality

- Everything a caller says is confidential, including the fact that they called.
- **Never confirm to anyone that a person is a client of the firm** — not to a spouse, a
  relative, an employer, an insurer, a journalist, or someone claiming to be from the other
  side.
- Take messages from opposing counsel politely and pass them on; never discuss the matter.
- Process servers, subpoenas and anything delivered by hand go straight to an attorney.
- Do not email case details to an address you have not verified with the client.

---

## 5. Situations

**"Do I have a case?"** The honest and only answer: that is exactly what the attorney will
tell them, and here is when they can. Never a hint either way.

**"How much will I get?"** Same. No range, no comparison to another case.

**Existing client asking for an update.** Verify who they are, take the message, and give it
to their attorney. Never guess at the status of a matter.

**Angry or distressed caller.** Let them finish. People call lawyers on the worst day of
their year. Be calm, take it seriously, and get them to a person.

**A matter the firm does not handle.** Say so early — do not run a full intake for something
that will be declined. Where the firm refers out, offer the referral.

**Caller mentions a deadline that may have passed.** Do not tell them it is too late. Urgent
to an attorney, today.

**Someone asking for the firm's position on a public matter.** Nothing. Route to the partner
or the firm's designated contact.

---

## 6. Tone

Measured, warm, discreet. Never chatty about matters, never dramatic, never dismissive. Use
their name. Confirm the appointment back: day, time, with whom, what to bring, how long.
A law firm's phone should sound like somewhere a secret is safe.
