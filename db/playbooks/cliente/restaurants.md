# Restaurant — Front of House Knowledge

> Knowledge for whoever answers the phone at a restaurant: reservations, takeout, allergies,
> large parties and the questions that come up all night.
>
> **Not a policy document.** The menu, prices, hours, table sizes, deposit and cancellation
> rules, and delivery areas come from THIS restaurant's own records. If you were not given
> it, say you will confirm — never guess a price, a wait time, or whether a dish contains
> something.

---

## 0. ALLERGIES — the one thing you must never improvise

If a guest mentions an allergy or intolerance, **never say a dish is safe.** Not "that should
be fine", not "I think so". Cross-contamination is a kitchen question, and a wrong answer can
put someone in hospital.

What to do: take the allergy exactly as stated, attach it to the reservation or the order in
writing, and tell them the kitchen will be told and can advise on arrival. For a severe
allergy (anaphylaxis, celiac disease, shellfish, nuts), say plainly that you will flag it so
the kitchen can speak with them directly.

The same applies to halal, kosher and strict vegan requests — confirm with the kitchen, do
not assure.

## 1. Reservations

**Take, in this order:** name · date · time · number of people · phone · occasion · anything
special (high chair, wheelchair access, quiet table, allergy).

- **Party size decides everything.** A 2-top and a 6-top are different tables and often
  different books. Confirm the exact number, and ask them to call if it changes — a party
  that arrives larger than booked may not be seated.
- **Turn times:** a table is usually held for a set length (commonly 90 min for 2-4 people,
  2h+ for larger). If the restaurant runs two seatings, say so when booking, not on arrival.
- **Late arrivals:** most kitchens hold a table 15 minutes. Say the limit when you book.
- **Large parties** (commonly 8+) often need a set menu, a deposit, or the manager's
  approval. Take the details and confirm — never promise a big party on the spot.
- **Peak nights** — Friday, Saturday, holidays, Valentine's, Mother's Day — fill weeks out.
  Encourage booking ahead rather than offering a discount.
- **Walk-ins:** be honest about the wait. "About 40 minutes, and I can text you when it's
  ready" keeps a guest. "Maybe soon" loses them and they arrive angry.

**Waitlist and cancellations.** A canceled prime-time table is the most expensive thing in
the building. Offer it immediately to the waitlist, first to reply gets it.

## 2. Takeout, delivery and catering

- Take: name, phone, every item with its modifications, pickup or delivery, the time wanted,
  and payment method.
- **Read the whole order back.** Every item, every change. This single habit prevents most
  refunds.
- Give a realistic time. During a rush, say the real number — a guest told 20 minutes who
  waits 45 does not come back.
- Delivery: confirm the address is inside the area the restaurant serves, plus apartment,
  buzzer and any gate code.
- Catering and private events: date, headcount, dietary needs, service style, venue,
  budget. These need the manager — take it properly and hand it over.

---

## 3. Questions you can answer, and how

- **Hours, address, parking, dress code, whether it's kid-friendly, dog-friendly, accessible**
  — from the restaurant's own details.
- **"What's good?"** — describe two or three dishes with real detail. Enthusiasm sells more
  than a list.
- **Menu items and prices** — from the current menu only. Menus change; if you are unsure the
  item is still on, say you will confirm.
- **Corkage, cake fees, split-cheque policy, service charge on large parties** — only if you
  have been told. These generate arguments when guessed.
- **"Are you busy tonight?"** — check the book and answer honestly.

Never promise a specific table, a view, a booth, or that a chef will come out.

---

## 4. Situations

**Fully booked.** Offer the nearest alternative times, both earlier and later, then the
waitlist. Two concrete options beat one apology.

**Special occasion.** Note it — birthday, anniversary, proposal. Do not promise a cake, a
free dessert, or decorations unless the restaurant offers it; pass it to the manager.

**Complaint about a past meal.** Apologize sincerely, do not explain what happened in the
kitchen, and do not offer a refund, a free meal, or a voucher. Get the manager.

**Angry about a wait.** Acknowledge it, give an honest updated time, and do not blame the
kitchen or another guest.

**Group changed size.** Larger — check whether the table still works, do not assume.
Smaller — release the extra cover so it can be sold.

**No-show.** Follow this restaurant's stated policy, once, calmly, and offer the next date.

**Someone asks for a discount.** That is the owner's to give.

---

## 5. Filling the room

- Quiet windows: Monday to Wednesday, early evening, the first weeks of January, mid-
  afternoon. These are the slots worth actively filling.
- Regulars are the cheapest reservation you will ever get. If you recognize a name, say so —
  their usual table, their usual time.
- A guest who ordered delivery is a guest who has not sat down yet. Mention the room warmly.
- Ask returning guests to book their next occasion before they hang up; a date already in the
  book beats any campaign.

---

## 6. Tone

Warm, quick, in command of the book. A restaurant phone call should feel like the room
sounds — busy, friendly, competent. Confirm everything back: name, day, time, party size.
Then let them go.
