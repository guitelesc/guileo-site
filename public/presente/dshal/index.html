I looked at dshalnyc.com today from the outside, the way a customer would. The salon is in good shape — the booking works, the menu is published, the site is fast on a phone. What is missing is the layer that machines read. Below is what I measured, and the markup itself, written for your salon, free to paste.

Measured today

There is no structured data anywhere on the site.

Zero application/ld+json blocks on the homepage and zero on the service menu. Google has no machine-readable statement that D'Shal is a hair salon in Gramercy, what it charges, when it opens, or where to book. Every competitor with that markup is eligible for results yours cannot appear in — and the AI assistants people now ask for salon recommendations read exactly this layer.

Measured today

Share your link and nothing shows up.

No meta description and no Open Graph tags on any page. When someone drops dshalnyc.com in a DM, a story or a group chat, it renders as naked text — no photo, no name, no line about the salon. For a business whose customers arrive from Instagram, that is the preview doing none of the work.

Measured today

The page title is 109 characters and repeats itself.

D'Shal Salon NYC – Blowout – Color – Keratin Treatments – Hair Blowouts – Colors – Keratin Treatments – 10010

“Blowout”, “Color” and “Keratin” each appear twice. Google shows roughly the first 60 characters, so what a searcher actually sees is the repetition. Something like D'Shal Salon — Hair Color, Keratin & Blowouts in Gramercy, NYC says more in less space.

Yours to keep

The markup, written for your salon.

Built from what is published on your own site: the name, the Gramercy address, the phone, the hours from your contact page, your Vagaro booking link, your Instagram, and the services you list.

I deliberately left the individual prices out. Your menu uses ranges and conditions (“for coarse hair / extensions”, “price upon consultation”) and I would rather hand you a range you can confirm than guess a number and put it in front of Google in your name.
