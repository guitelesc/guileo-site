# How Much Money Your Business Loses on Missed Calls (and How to Stop It)

A ringing phone is the sound of someone ready to spend money. When nobody answers, that someone doesn't leave a polite note and wait — they call the next business on the list. For local service businesses, missed calls aren't a minor annoyance. They're a slow, invisible leak in your revenue that almost never shows up in any report, because you can't measure a customer you never knew you had.

This article breaks down where missed calls come from, how to estimate what they're actually costing you, and how a modern AI receptionist closes the gap.

## Why Missed Calls Are More Common Than You Think

Most owners assume they answer "most" of their calls. The reality is usually worse. Industry estimates have long suggested that a large share of inbound calls to small businesses go unanswered — and during busy periods that share climbs sharply.

Think about a typical day. You're with a customer, on a ladder, driving between jobs, or simply at lunch. The phone rings and there's no one free to pick it up. Now multiply that across:

- **The after-hours gap.** Plenty of people search and call in the evening, after they get off work. If your hours end at 5 or 6, those calls hit voicemail.
- **The lunch gap.** The midday rush is real, and it's exactly when small teams are stretched thinnest.
- **The weekend gap.** Saturday and Sunday are prime decision-making time for homeowners, patients, and shoppers — and prime time for closed offices.

The first business to actually answer tends to win the job. Studies of buyer behavior consistently point to the same pattern: the company that responds first has a strong advantage, often capturing the majority of deals simply by being available when the customer is ready. If your competitor answers and you don't, the decision is frequently already made before you ever call back.

## The Math: Putting a Dollar Figure on Missed Calls

You don't need fancy analytics to estimate the damage. You need three numbers you already know or can reasonably guess:

1. **Missed calls per month.** Check your phone records or call your carrier. Even a modest small business often misses dozens.
2. **Your close rate.** Of the people who reach you and are a real fit, what fraction become customers? Many service businesses land somewhere between 20% and 50%.
3. **Your average ticket (or customer lifetime value).** What is one new customer worth — the first job, or everything they spend over time?

The formula is simple:

> **Missed calls/month × Close rate × Average ticket = Estimated monthly revenue lost**

A worked example, framed as an estimate, not a promise:

- 50 missed calls per month
- 30% close rate
- $400 average ticket

That's `50 × 0.30 × $400 = $6,000` per month in potential revenue walking out the door — roughly **$72,000 a year**. Even if you're more conservative and assume only half those callers would have bought, you're still looking at tens of thousands annually.

The point isn't the exact figure. The point is that the number is almost always far larger than owners expect, and it's recurring. Plug in your own three numbers and the picture gets very real, very fast.

## The First Fix: Missed-Call Text-Back

The single cheapest improvement is to never leave a missed call completely silent. With **missed-call text-back**, the moment a call goes unanswered, the caller automatically gets a friendly text:

> "Sorry we missed you! This is [Business]. How can we help? Reply here and we'll get right back to you."

This does two things. It tells the caller you exist and you care — so they're less likely to immediately dial a competitor — and it opens a text conversation, which many people actually prefer over waiting on hold. A text-back won't close the deal by itself, but it buys you time and keeps the lead warm instead of cold.
