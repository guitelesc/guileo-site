# Speed-to-Lead: Why Answering in 60 Seconds Wins the Deal

Most businesses think they lose deals on price, on features, or on reputation. More often, they lose on a stopwatch. The moment a new lead raises their hand — a form fill, a call, a "send me info" — a clock starts. The business that responds first usually walks away with the sale, and the gap between first and second place is measured in minutes, sometimes seconds.

This is **speed-to-lead**: how fast you respond after someone shows interest. It's one of the highest-leverage numbers in your entire operation, and it's almost always overlooked.

## The 60-Second and 5-Minute Rules

Two thresholds matter, and both are brutal.

The widely cited research on lead response found that contacting a lead within **5 minutes** versus 30 minutes makes you dramatically more likely to actually connect and qualify them. Wait longer, and your odds of even reaching that person — let alone closing — fall off a cliff. After an hour, you're often calling a colder, distracted prospect who has already moved on to someone else.

But 5 minutes is the old standard. The new bar is **under 60 seconds**. When you respond within a minute, the lead is still on your website, still thinking about their problem, still in buying mode. You're not interrupting their day later — you're meeting them exactly when they decided to act.

The payoff is concrete: studies of buyer behavior consistently show that the first company to respond captures roughly **78% of deals**. Not because they're better, cheaper, or smarter — simply because they got there first while the customer was ready to talk.

## Why First Responder Wins

A few human truths drive this:

- **Urgency is perishable.** When someone reaches out, they're motivated *right now*. That motivation decays by the hour.
- **The first helpful voice anchors trust.** Whoever answers first gets to frame the conversation, build rapport, and set the criteria the customer uses to judge everyone else.
- **People hate repeating themselves.** Once a prospect has explained their situation to one responsive business, calling around again feels like work. The first good answer often ends the search.
- **Silence reads as indifference.** A slow reply doesn't say "we're busy" to a customer — it says "they don't want my business."

## What Kills Slow Follow-Up

If speed is so powerful, why is most follow-up slow? The usual culprits:

- **Leads arrive when you're unavailable** — on a job, in a meeting, asleep, on the weekend.
- **Manual hand-offs.** A form goes to an inbox, the inbox gets checked "later," and later is too late.
- **No system, just willpower.** Relying on someone remembering to call back is a plan that fails on the busiest days — which are also the days with the most leads.
- **Business hours that don't match buyer hours.** People research and decide on nights and weekends, exactly when most offices are dark.

The result is a predictable leak: leads come in fast and get answered slow, and competitors quietly collect the difference.

## Speed-to-Lead by Vertical

The principle is universal, but it plays out differently across industries:

### Home Services (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing)
Emergencies don't wait. A homeowner with a flooding basement calls three companies and hires the first human who picks up. Instant answer and same-minute callback aren't a nicety here — they're the entire game.

### Real Estate
Online property inquiries go stale in minutes. A buyer who fills out a form is usually filling out several. The agent who calls back in under a minute — while the listing is still on their screen — gets the showing. Everyone else gets voicemail tag.

### Clinics and Practices (dental, medical, aesthetic, therapy)
New patients often book when something hurts or when they finally have a free moment at night. An instant, reassuring response that gets them on the calendar beats a callback two days later, when the urgency — and the patient — has faded. Faster booking also means fewer empty chairs.
